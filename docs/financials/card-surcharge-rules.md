# Card surcharges ("customer pays the card fee"): rules and how Built2Bid enforces them

Researched 2026-11-21 against statutes, attorney general / regulator guidance and court decisions. This is a
compliance design, not legal advice. Counsel should review, especially Texas, California, Oklahoma, New York and
New Jersey. Code: `src/lib/financials/surcharge.ts` (engine) and `surcharge-rules.ts` (this table as data).

## What Built2Bid enforces on every payment

1. **Both states.** For card-not-present payments no court has settled whether the merchant's or cardholder's state
   governs, so we apply both: the contractor's business state (Financials → Tax) and the customer's state (from the
   invoice address, or chosen on the pay page), and again the card's **billing state** at capture. Banned or
   contested in either → no surcharge.
2. **Credit only.** Visa, Mastercard and Louisiana (from 2026-08-01), Colorado and Maine bar debit surcharges. The
   surcharge is authorized with the payment but captured only after Stripe reports `card.funding = credit` with a US
   billing address in an allowed state. Debit, prepaid, unknown funding, or wallets that hide funding → we capture
   only the invoice amount.
3. **Cap.** The lowest of: 3% (Visa's cap, the lowest network cap; Mastercard is 4% today), the state cap (CO 2%,
   OK 2%, MN 5%), the contractor's chosen rate, and the contractor's **actual card cost** on the surcharged total
   (their rate, default Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢). NY, NJ, GA, SD and the networks all cap at cost.
4. **Avoidable.** Bank (ACH) must stay on with no surcharge, so the fee is always optional (Oklahoma requires another
   payment method; all-in pricing laws in CO, VA, OR, RI, DC, CA and MA treat only avoidable fees as optional).
5. **Disclosure before payment.** Pay page shows amount due, surcharge and card total side by side, with the
   disclosure; Stripe Checkout shows the surcharge as its own line plus the disclosure by the Pay button; the invoice
   PDF/preview prints the disclosure with the card total and the bank/debit total (New York's total-price rule).
   Colorado's statutory notice is added for Colorado payments.
6. **30 days' notice.** Visa requires the merchant to notify its acquirer (Stripe) 30 days ahead; Mastercard's rule
   is the same (registration currently paused). The contractor enters the notice date and accepts the terms;
   surcharging can't start until 30 days later (checked in the API and in the database).
7. **Accounting.** The invoice payment is recorded at the invoice amount. The surcharge actually captured is stored
   in `fin_online_payments.surcharge_amount` and the ledger posts it against merchant fees, never as customer credit.
8. **Kill switch.** Netlify `CONNECT_CARD_SURCHARGE=off` turns surcharging off everywhere.

## Card network and federal rules (summary)

- **Visa** (Merchant Surcharge Q&A, Feb 2024): credit only; lesser of MDR or 3% (cut from 4% on 2023-04-15);
  notify acquirer 30 days ahead; disclose at point of entry and point of sale; separate line on every receipt;
  brand or product level, not both.
- **Mastercard:** lesser of MDR or 4%; 30-day notice to acquirer (registration paused pending revised rules).
- **Amex:** a surcharge may not exceed what other card brands are charged; discounts for ACH/cash allowed.
- **Visa/Mastercard settlement** (EDNY 05-md-1720): preliminary approval 2026-06-09, fairness hearing 2026-11-16,
  **no ruling yet**. Would set a 3% cap for both networks. Nothing is in effect.
- **Federal:** no federal statute bans surcharges (the TILA ban expired 1984); discounts for cash/ACH/debit are
  protected (15 U.S.C. 1666f, 1693o-2(b)(2)). The FTC junk-fee rule covers only tickets and lodging.
- **Convenience fees** don't fit: Visa requires a flat fee for a true alternative channel; online invoices are the
  contractor's main card channel.

## Known limits and follow-ups

- Funding is checked after authorization, so a debit cardholder briefly sees a hold for invoice + surcharge until
  capture (seconds, via the webhook). Stripe's surcharge API (public preview) or a Payment Element build could
  decide before authorization; revisit when it is generally available.
- States marked medium/low confidence rely on official guidance or several agreeing secondary sources.
- Sales tax on surcharges (e.g. Michigan treats them as taxable) is not yet computed.
- Re-check this table at least quarterly and whenever the settlement gets a final ruling.
